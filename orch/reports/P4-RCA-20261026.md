## 1. Headline

**P-4 fails because of how the metric is defined, interacting with what the policy is actually learning. It is not a return collapse, and the pipeline is OK.**

1. **`norm_return` is a per-step average, not a cumulative return.**
   - In all 12 eval JSONs (2 attempts × 2 arms × 3 masks), `summary.step_weighted_norm_return` equals Σ(per-step reward-term means) / 0.1 to 4 decimal places.
   - Example, a2 attention mask 0.00: the terms sum to 0.031087, and 0.031087 / 0.1 = 0.3109. The JSON field is 0.31087.
   - Per clip, a2 attention dance1: the terms sum to 0.030109, giving 0.3011. The JSON field is 0.30109.
   - The divisor 0.1 is the maximum per-step reward: positive reward weights 0.5+0.5+1+1+1+1 = 5.0 (RewardManager table in the train log) × env step 0.02 s.
   - So `norm_return` = (mean reward per step) / (max reward per step). Episode length does not enter it.
   - The train scalar `train/episode_norm_return` has the same name and the same value range, and it moves opposite to `train/episode_len` (point 3). The upstream train-side code is not on the box (cloned on Colab only, `code/vendor/NOTES.md`). So the train-side definition is **inferred, not read from source**. That is the one evidence gap; option C1 closes it.
2. **The P-4 baseline is effectively a policy that does almost nothing.**
   - At the first logged point (it 50) only `time/updates` = 4 policy updates have run.
   - `policy/action_abs_mean` there is 0.0016 (attention) and 0.0005 (mlp_matched).
   - Policy updates begin at about it 40: updates go 4 → 24 between it 50 and it 100, i.e. 0.4 per iteration, which extrapolates to zero at it 40. Upstream applies some warmup before policy updates. The PLAN does not define it numerically.
   - That near-zero-action policy holds the default pose from a reference-state start. It falls after about 30 steps (`episode_len` 29.94) but tracks well while it stands: `norm_return` 0.3838 / 0.3839.
   - So the P-4 baseline is a short episode with a high per-step average.
3. **Learning lengthens episodes, which lowers the per-step average.**

   | run | nret it50 → last | elen it50 → last | raw-return proxy nret×elen×0.1 | corr(nret, elen) over 27–28 points |
   |---|---|---|---|---|
   | a1 attention | 0.3838 → 0.3150 (−17.9%) | 29.94 → 65.02 (+117%) | 1.149 → 2.048 (**+78%**) | −0.916 |
   | a2 attention | 0.3838 → 0.3048 (−20.6%) | 29.94 → 67.29 (+125%) | 1.149 → 2.051 (**+78%**) | −0.932 |
   | a1 mlp_matched | 0.3839 → 0.3572 (−7.0%) | 29.93 → 37.86 (+27%) | 1.149 → 1.352 (**+18%**) | −0.044 |
   | a2 mlp_matched | 0.3839 → 0.3507 (−8.6%) | 29.93 → 39.29 (+31%) | 1.149 → 1.378 (**+20%**) | −0.160 |

   - The proxy is a product of window means, not a mean of per-episode products. It is an approximation, but its direction is unambiguous.
   - Episodes completed per 50-iteration window (`n=` in the train log) fall as episodes get longer, e.g. a2 attention n=803 at it 100 → n=298 at it 1350. This is consistent with longer survival.
4. **Every other learning signal improves in both arms and both attempts.** Values are it 50 → last, a2:

   | signal | attention | mlp_matched |
   |---|---|---|
   | `wm/loss` (P-3 passed) | 58.29 → 4.06 | 58.47 → 1.91 |
   | `policy/imag_return_undisc` | 0.098 → 0.379 | 0.098 → 0.389 |
   | `policy/imag_survival_H` | 0.0002 → 0.674 | 0.0002 → 0.563 |
   | `policy/critic_value_mean` | 0.0006 → 0.958 | 0.0097 → 0.389 |

   - `wm/term_acc` rises from 0.989 to 0.998.
   - No NaN (P-2 passed). `policy_grad_norm` stays ≤ 0.145.

   This rules out a learning collapse.

**Classification (question 4): mostly a metric artifact of the per-step `norm_return` combined with a P-4 that is too strict, and wrongly anchored, for a 0.5 h smoke. The pipeline is OK. Underneath sit two real, non-pathological behaviours:**
- **(a) Attention trades smoothness for survival.** The `action_rate_l2` penalty explains about half of its per-step drop (§3).
- **(b) The policy is still far from good tracking at 0.5 h.** Every eval clip ends early.

Neither is a collapse.

## 2. Question 2 — same failure mode in both arms?

**Same mechanism, different strength. Both attempts reproduce it almost exactly.**

- **Reproducibility.** a1 and a2 agree to 4 decimals at it 50 (attention nret 0.38384 / 0.38384; mlp 0.38390 / 0.38391) and stay within about 0.01 throughout. This is systematic, not chance; a third identical attempt would very probably FAIL on P-4 again.
- **Attention arm: survival-driven.**
  - nret and elen are strongly anti-correlated (−0.92 / −0.93).
  - elen rises steadily from about 40 (it 700) to 65–67 (it 1350), and nret falls in step.
  - Action magnitude `policy/action_abs_mean` reaches 0.61–0.62.
  - The `action_rate_l2` penalty in imagination grows to −0.0035 / −0.0038 per step, i.e. **−0.035 / −0.038 in norm units**. That is about half of the 0.069 / 0.079 nret drop. This is an estimate from imagined-reward terms, not from real-env terms.
- **mlp_matched arm: an early dip that recovers, with an endpoint dip.**
  - elen stays flat at about 32–34 from it 100 to it 1150.
  - At that fixed length nret dips while actions ramp up (minimum 0.3428 / 0.3466 at it 300). It then **recovers to 0.3832 / 0.3835 at it 1200**, i.e. per-step tracking improves by about 12% at constant episode length.
  - Over the last 4 points, elen rises to 37.9 / 39.3 and nret falls to 0.357 / 0.351.
  - Weak correlation (−0.04 / −0.16): the endpoint FAIL here is partly endpoint sensitivity. The run maximum after warmup (0.3840 at it 100 in a1) is essentially equal to the baseline.
- **Eval shows the same trade-off between arms** (reported only, no threshold; masks 0.00 / 0.10 / 0.20):

  | run | eval norm_return | summed survival steps (4 clips) | raw-return proxy Σ |
  |---|---|---|---|
  | a2 attention | 0.330 / 0.314 / 0.354 | 720 / 546 / 576 | 22.4 / 17.5 / 20.4 |
  | a2 mlp_matched | 0.421 / 0.433 / 0.428 | 196 / 211 / 206 | 8.3 / 9.2 / 8.8 |
  | a1 attention | — | 1112 / 496 / 450 | 40.3 / 16.6 / 16.2 |
  | a1 mlp_matched | — | 203 / 199 / 198 | 9.4 / 9.0 / 8.8 |

  - On `norm_return`, MLP "wins". On survival or cumulative reward, attention wins by 2–5×.
  - **The headline B0 comparison metric therefore has the same artifact.** That matters beyond P-4 (see B3).
- **Upstream sanity shows the mirror image.** For `up_sanity` (unmodified upstream decoder, 0.1 h, 256 envs):
  - nret rises 0.3919 → 0.4148 / 0.4295.
  - elen falls 29.77 → 22.53 / 23.10.
  - The raw-return proxy falls −20% / −15%.

  The upstream decoder at 0.1 h dies sooner and scores a higher per-step average. Two consequences:
  - Had P-4 applied to `up_sanity`, it would "pass" while survival got worse.
  - A cumulative-return criterion would also be noisy over the first ~100 updates.

  Confound: `up_sanity` uses a different `num_envs` and cap from the arms.

## 3. Question 3 — definitions, warmup, smoke length, eval termination

- **Reward and return logging.**
  - Eval `norm_return` = per-step mean / 0.1, proven by arithmetic in §1.1.
  - `mean_norm_return` averages clips equally; `step_weighted_norm_return` weights clips by steps.
  - Neither contains episode length. Train `episode_norm_return` is inferred to match; source unread (C1).
  - The tb reward channels are imagination-only (`policy/imag_r/*`). No real-env per-term train series is logged, so the per-term attribution in §2 is approximate.
- **Penalty terms.**
  - Eval `action_rate_l2` / 0.1 is −0.043 / −0.088 / −0.109 for a2 attention across masks, versus −0.004 / −0.009 / −0.011 for a2 mlp.
  - It is about 0 for the baseline policy, whose |a| is about 0.
  - Any learned policy pays this penalty from the start, so a per-step baseline taken from a policy with |a|≈0 is biased upward.
- **Warmup ambiguity in the PLAN.**
  - PLAN §5 says "first logged point after warmup", with no number.
  - The verdict used the first point with `time/updates > 0` (it 50), which contains mostly episodes from before any update.
  - Any anchor from it 50 to it 100 gives about 0.3835, and P-4 fails for all 4 runs.
  - Post-hoc sensitivity (not a recommendation): anchoring at each run's post-ramp minimum (it 300) would pass mlp_matched (0.3572 > 0.3428; 0.3507 > 0.3466) and still fail attention (0.3150 < 0.3658; 0.3048 < 0.3682).
  - Averaging the first 5 and last 5 points from it 50 still fails all 4: attention 0.3762 → 0.3285 / 0.3775 → 0.3126; mlp 0.3734 → 0.3683 / 0.3734 → 0.3655.
  - Starting the first-5 window at updates ≥ 20 (it 100–300) gives mlp a coin-flip (a1 0.3652 → 0.3683 pass; a2 0.3660 → 0.3655 fail). Attention fails clearly either way (§4 B2).
  - So the warmup ambiguity alone does not explain the FAIL. The per-step definition does.
- **Smoke length against hyperparameters.**
  - 0.5 h on an L4 gives 524 / 544 policy updates at 0.7 it/s.
  - Time per 50 iterations is dominated by the policy step: sim / wm / pi = 1.8 / 9.8 / 58 s.
  - Settings are 512 envs and horizon 16, against the paper's 4096 envs and horizon 32 (`configs/colab_smoke.yaml`).
  - At this budget the policy is just learning to stay up (elen 30 → 39–67 steps, i.e. 0.6 → 0.8–1.3 s). Expecting per-step tracking quality to beat a do-nothing start on the first ~0.6 s of each episode is not a reasonable smoke-level expectation.
  - Note that P-4 as written passes in a regime where the policy dies *faster*, as `up_sanity` shows.
- **Eval early termination.**
  - All 4 clips fail early in all 12 eval files.
  - Attention: fail steps 89–379 (≈1.8–7.6 s).
  - mlp_matched: fail steps 42–66 (≈0.8–1.3 s).
  - The clips are 6574–13066 frames long.
  - This is consistent with an under-trained smoke policy, not a pipeline fault: eval loads `ckpt_latest.pt`, writes all 3 masks, and S1–S5 hold. The termination terms are `root_height` and `root_tilt` (non-timeout), as in the train log TerminationManager table.

### B) PLAN amendment candidates for a future attempt (Class 2: human lead signs before the attempt starts; text proposed, NOT applied)

These are chosen after seeing the data, so any adopted text must say so under Confounds.

**B1. Replace the P-4 metric with a cumulative or survival measure.** Proposed text:
> "P-4 (amended): for each arm, mean `train/episode_len` × `train/episode_norm_return` (cumulative normalised return per episode) over the last 5 logged points > the same quantity over the first 5 logged points with `time/updates ≥ 20`. `train/episode_norm_return` alone is reported, no threshold."

  - Would pass all 4 runs. Raw proxy, last 5 vs first 5 points with updates ≥ 20 (it 100–300): 1.922 vs 1.233 (a1 att), 1.890 vs 1.220 (a2 att), 1.347 vs 1.180 (a1 mlp), 1.362 vs 1.191 (a2 mlp).
  - Justified by the definition in §1.1, not by the fact that it passes.

**B2. Define warmup numerically and smooth the endpoints.** Proposed text:
> "'after warmup' = first logged point with `time/updates ≥ 20` (≈ it 100 at log-interval 50); compare means of the first 5 and last 5 logged points."

  - On its own this **does not fix P-4**. nret means, first 5 points with updates ≥ 20 vs last 5:
    - attention: 0.3726 → 0.3285 (a1) and 0.3744 → 0.3126 (a2). FAIL.
    - mlp_matched: 0.3652 → 0.3683 (a1, marginal pass) and 0.3660 → 0.3655 (a2, marginal fail). The verdict would flip on noise.
  - Useful only together with B1 or B4.

**B3. Use cumulative or step-weighted eval reporting for the arm comparison (affects B0/P3, not only P-4).** Proposed text:
> "Eval reports per arm/mask: Σ steps survived, Σ (norm_return × steps), and mean_norm_return. No single per-step metric is used to rank arms."

  - Evidence: on per-step return, mlp_matched leads attention (0.42 vs 0.33). On survival or cumulative reward, attention leads by 2–5× (§2 eval table).

**B4. Longer cap or relative criterion.** Two candidates:
  - (a) Raise `--schedule.max-hours` to 1.5–2.0 h for one arm-pair. The cost is roughly 3–4× Colab time, and §2 does not show that per-step nret recovers within that span for attention.
  - (b) Replace P-4 with "P-4: reported, no threshold" and keep P-3 as the only learning gate.
  - B1 is preferred over B4a: it is cheaper, and its justification comes from the metric definition rather than hoping the curve turns.

### C) Change-request candidates against the P1 freeze (propose only; NOT applied; each needs a change request and a new tarball hash)
